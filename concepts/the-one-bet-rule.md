# The one-bet rule

Kian's explicit operating rule, distilled from his own multi-year journal pattern.

## The rule

- Keep **one primary bet** at a time
- Lock it for a real interval (typically 30 days)
- Judge it by **compounding**, not by day-to-day mood
- Explore deliberately, not passively
- Revisit only on schedule, not whenever uncertainty appears

## The pattern this fights

- Kian repeatedly sees that depth, singular focus, and long-term commitment create compounding returns
- He also repeatedly hesitates to commit because he wants optionality, doesn't yet feel total certainty, or suspects the world may change before the bet pays off
- He then looks backward and wishes he had locked in earlier

## The synthesis (verbatim from `commitment-to-one-goal.md`)

- Optionality is expensive when it prevents expertise.
- Regret usually comes less from choosing wrongly than from failing to commit hard enough for long enough.
- The right unit of commitment is not necessarily "10 years immediately"; it is often a sequence of honest medium-length locks that are long enough to generate compounding.
- If there is no obsession yet, the correct move is not endless drift; it is structured exploration with real commitment windows.
